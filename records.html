Published · PLOS One (2025)

Forecasting global temperature with LSTM and ARIMA

First-listed co-first author

Applying deep learning and time-series forecasting to 142 years of climate data, this study decodes the complex interplay between greenhouse emissions, the COVID-19 economic shock, and global temperature trends to inform sustainable policy-making.

Methods

LSTM · ARIMA · Pearson & Spearman Correlation · Time-Series Forecasting

Peer-reviewed publication · First Prize, Asia–Pacific MCM

Research contributions

  • Implemented forecasting software and analysed 1880–2022 global temperature data using LSTM and ARIMA within the co-authored study.
  • Used Pearson and Spearman correlations to examine associations between greenhouse gases and temperature, including pandemic-period patterns. These analyses do not isolate a causal pandemic effect.
  • Connected the quantitative results with environmental-management questions and contributed original drafting and revision, keeping model projections distinct from observed changes.

The published author-contribution statement credits TAN Can with data curation, formal analysis, investigation, resources, software, original drafting and revision. Methodology is credited to Junyi Zhong.

The emissions and pandemic analyses are associational; forecasting and correlation do not establish causal effects.

Healthcare investment efficiency in China

Third author · Quantitative research contributor

Evaluating a decade of national healthcare data to optimize resource allocation and measure the true efficiency of medical inputs versus outcomes.

Methods

Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) · Efficiency Evaluation · Longitudinal Data Analysis · Origin

Finance Research Letters 67, 105688

Research contributions

  • Designed a robust input-output evaluation index system to accurately measure and quantify the true efficiency of healthcare resource allocation.
  • Processed and modeled a 10-year longitudinal dataset (2012–2021) of China's healthcare system using DEA to identify regional efficiency bottlenecks.
  • Produced publication-ready visual assets using Origin, translating complex efficiency matrices into intuitive charts that directly supported the paper's core arguments.

The published CRediT statement credits TAN Can with original drafting, software, methodology, investigation, formal analysis, data curation and conceptualisation. This statement is available in the original published PDF.

An independent study of whether China’s environmental fee-to-tax reform changes corporate default risk. The study constructs KMV–EDF risk measures and uses firm panel data with DID / DDD, PSM-DID and DDML.

Methods

KMV–EDF · Causal Inference · DID / DDD · PSM-DID · DDML · Stata

Outstanding Undergraduate Thesis · 96/100 · School rank 1st

Research contributions

  • Constructed corporate default-risk measures with the KMV–EDF framework and prepared a panel dataset for policy evaluation.
  • Examined the environmental fee-to-tax reform using DID / DDD, PSM-DID and Double Machine Learning, connecting environmental regulation with corporate financial risk.
